\section{Conclusion}
A 350M hybrid convolution-attention backbone, fitted with a joint schema head,
is a viable System-One decision engine: 81.3\%/72.9\% in-distribution means
over three seeds, 44\%/55.6\% held-out-taxonomy transfer, and honest,
reproduced numbers throughout. Our main methodological finding is a cautionary
one: a dramatic augmentation effect was context truncation in disguise, and
calibration fit in-distribution collapses under shift even as error detection
survives. Artifacts---seven training runs, six evaluation kernels, per-item
dumps, and logs---are archived for reproduction. Checkpoints (adapters, head,
merged weights) plus \texttt{run\_meta.json} live in per-run kernel outputs;
lightweight results (\texttt{eval\_results.json}, probability dumps, metric
tables) are versioned in \texttt{docs/eval/}, and every paper number traces to
a logged run. The immediate next steps are separating augmentation types
(rename vs paraphrase vs derived) to attribute the transfer credit, adding
OOS/OOD and macro-F1 leaderboard slices, and replacing single post-hoc
temperatures with shift-aware calibration (per-domain temperatures or a
proper-loss RLCD stage~\cite{gao2026openjev}). Future work separates
augmentation types, extends OOS/OOD and leaderboard slices, and explores
shift-aware calibration.
