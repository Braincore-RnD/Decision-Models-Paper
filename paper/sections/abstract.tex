\begin{abstract}
Generative language models are increasingly misused as structured decision
routers, paying $O(T)$ sequential decoding cost and returning uncalibrated
confidences. System-One decision models instead map a state and a typed schema
to probabilities in a single forward pass. We adapt
LiquidAI LFM2.5-350M, a hybrid convolution-attention model, into a decision
engine with a cross-attention JointSchemaHead trained via QLoRA. Across seven
controlled training runs and six evaluation kernels we find: (1) leave-one-
taxonomy-out transfer is substantial but partial---43.4\% on BANKING77 and
55.6\% on CLINC150 against untrained-head baselines of 2.4\%/17.2\% (uniform
chance 1.3\%/0.67\%), versus 81.3\%/72.9\% in-distribution
means over three seeds---but at or below a zero-shot embedding baseline
(68.0\%/56.9\% full-test with no training); (2) an earlier 36\%\,$\to$\,83\% augmentation jump was a
context-truncation confound, not semantic robustness: with row-matched budgets,
canonical training wins in-distribution (86\%/84\% vs 80\%/70\%) while
augmentation coincides with transfer and memory savings; (3) calibration does not
transfer---ECE rises from 0.026 in-distribution to 0.366 under shift, and
in-distribution temperatures recover only part of it (0.220 vs 0.104 oracle)---yet
error-detection (AUROC 0.87\,$\to$\,0.73) survives, and realistic autonomous
coverage collapses from 63\% to near zero. All checkpoints, dumps, and logs are
archived for reproduction.
\end{abstract}
