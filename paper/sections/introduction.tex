\section{Introduction}
Enterprise automation---fraud routing, ticket triage, policy enforcement---needs
decisions that are fast, syntactically valid, and uncertainty-aware. The dominant
practice prompts an autoregressive model to emit JSON, incurring three costs:
(i)~$O(T)$ sequential decoding (measured 0.18\,s per attempt with
$\sim$100\% schema violation on our backbone, Section~6),
(ii)~non-zero schema-violation rates under constrained budgets, and
(iii)~systematic overconfidence from preference-based alignment, which makes raw
softmax scores unusable for risk-controlled automation.

System-One decision models~\cite{typesafe-jev,cloudflare-clef,convai-laya},
inspired by dual-process theory~\cite{kahneman2011}, separate deciding from
generating: a state $S$ and a typed schema $Q$ are mapped to a joint
distribution over options in one forward pass ($O(1)$ decoding steps), with
probabilities trained to be calibrated. Open questions remain for sub-billion
models: does schema augmentation drive transfer to unseen taxonomies, what does
data-mixture composition trade off at fixed capacity, and does calibration
survive distribution shift?

We study these on LiquidAI LFM2.5-350M, a hybrid model with convolutional
 sequence mixers and grouped-query attention, fitted with a cross-attention
 JointSchemaHead via QLoRA, on the BANKING77~\cite{casanueva2020banking77} and
 CLINC150~\cite{larson2019clinc150} intent taxonomies. Our contributions, each backed by controlled runs
 with fixed test sets, are:
 \begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Measured transfer:} leave-one-taxonomy-out evaluation gives 43.4\%
   [39--48] on BANKING77 and 55.6\% on CLINC150 (untrained-head baselines 2.4\%/17.2\%;
   uniform chance 1.3\%/0.67\%) versus
   in-distribution means of 81.3\%/72.9\% over three seeds---but at or below a
   zero-shot embedding baseline (68.0\%/56.9\% full-test, no training).
   Against canonical (unaugmented) LOTO training (35.4\% [31--39] / 54.8\%
   [50--59]), augmentation buys $+$8.6\,pt on BANKING77 and nothing on CLINC150:
   the transfer credit is per-bench, not a headline.
 \item \textbf{A confound found and fixed:} an earlier 36\%\,$\to$\,83\%
   augmentation jump was context truncation ($2048$ tokens cutting wide
   schemas), not semantic robustness. The row-matched ablation is a null
   result (typed-ext 0.563$\pm$0.033 vs 0.549$\pm$0.011; 1.4\,pt inside
   7.6\,pt seed spread): in-distribution canonical and augmented training are
   indistinguishable from seed noise. Augmentation's verified role is transfer
   (item~1) plus memory savings at a small in-domain tax.
 \item \textbf{Calibration under shift:} ECE rises from 0.026 in-distribution to
   0.366 on the held-out taxonomy while error-detection AUROC only falls from
   0.87 to 0.73; autonomous coverage at 5\% risk collapses from 63\% to near
   zero. Temperature scaling fit in-distribution transfers only partially
   (0.366\,$\to$\,0.220 vs 0.104 oracle), consistent with the dataset-shift
   calibration degradation documented since~\cite{ovadia2019trust}; what is new
   here is the dissociation surviving inside one matched recipe (AUROC intact
   while ECE collapses) plus a calibrated slice: with 1\,000 calibration items
   ($T{=}1.72$) ECE falls 0.119\,$\to$\,0.039 on BANKING77 and transfers to
   CLINC150 (0.091\,$\to$\,0.048).
 \item \textbf{Artifacts:} ten training runs and eight evaluation dumps with
   checkpoints, per-item dumps, and logs archived for reproduction.
 \end{enumerate}
