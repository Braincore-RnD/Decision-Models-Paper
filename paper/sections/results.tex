\label{sec:results}
\subsection{In-Distribution: Three Seeds}
On identical tests, the base recipe gives typed-decisions 55.9/53.3/55.6
(mean 54.9$\pm$1.2), BANKING77 76.8/83.4/83.8 (mean 81.3$\pm$3.2), and CLINC150
68.2/76.6/73.8 (mean 72.9$\pm$3.5; population std, $n{=}3$). Seed spread on BANKING77 (76.8 vs $\sim$83.5) is

(1.3\% for 77 classes, 0.67\% for 150), evidence of a lexical prior that works
without training. Section~6 reports transfer against these untrained-head

\subsection{Transfer: Leave-One-Taxonomy-Out}
With BANKING77 fully held out, accuracy reaches \textbf{43.4\% [39--48\%]}
against a 2.4\% untrained-head baseline (uniform chance 1.3\%; paraphrase median
42.8\%, range 42.2--43.6); with CLINC150
held out, \textbf{55.6\%} (paraphrase 55.6--57.0) against a 17.2\% untrained-head
baseline (uniform chance 0.67\%). Transfer
is partial versus 81.3\%/72.9\% in-distribution---and against the honest
zero-shot floor it is weak: cosine similarity with bge-small over label names
alone (no training, no descriptions) reaches 68.0\% on full BANKING77 and
56.9\% on full CLINC150, i.e.\ at or above our transfer numbers (43.4/55.6).
The model beats that baseline only in-distribution (75.6/67.6 full-test).
Subset draws use the same seed value with different samplers, so subset
comparisons are approximate; full-test ones are exact.

Transfer is wording-robust, not prompt luck: a grid of canonical plus 2
variants (instruction
rewording plus option-order shuffling; intent benches only---typed records
carry fixed 5-question schemas and are not paraphrased) moves LOTO BANKING77 only across
42.2--43.6\% (median 42.8\%) and LOTO CLINC150 across 55.6--57.0\%, while
in-distribution A holds 86.0--86.4\% on BANKING77. Ranges this tight mean the
effect lives in schema semantics, not phrasing.

Augmentation's transfer credit is now measured per bench. Canonical (A) LOTO
training gives BANKING77 35.4\% [31--39] and CLINC150 54.8\% [50--59]
(500 items each, 2\,000 bootstraps; dumps
\texttt{docs/eval/kaggle/A-loto/}) against augmented (E) LOTO transfer of
44\% [38--47] and 55.6\%: augmentation buys $+$8.6\,pt on BANKING77 (CIs
nearly disjoint) and nothing on CLINC150. The headline ``augmentation buys
transfer'' holds per-bench only.

An earlier 36\%\,$\to$\,83\% jump attributed to augmentation was a context
truncation confound: training at 2\,048 tokens cut wide schemas, and
augmentation's option subsampling merely made them fit. With fitting context,
the in-distribution comparison is a null result: three-seed typed-ext gives A
0.563$\pm$0.033 vs E 0.549$\pm$0.011, a 1.4\,pt gap inside A's own 7.6\,pt
seed spread---indistinguishable from seed noise, so ``canonical wins'' does
not hold. Augmentation's verified role is transfer (per-bench, above) plus memory
savings (full 77/150-option schemas OOM at batch 8 on T4) at a small in-domain
tax. Residual confounds disclosed: +25\% decisions for E (biasing toward E) and
batch 4 vs 8 at equal effective size 32.

Overfitting dynamics are strikingly consistent: eval-loss spikes at epoch 3 in
every fully-logged run, so the restored best checkpoint is epoch 1 or 2, never
3 (Table~\ref{tab:overfit}; Figure~\ref{fig:loss}). The 700M run is the
exception that sharpens the rule: best at epoch 1 (0.577\,$\to$\,0.596\,$\to$\,0.718),
so capacity shifts the peak one epoch earlier.

\subsection{Calibration Under Shift}
On BANKING77, ECE rises from \textbf{0.026} in-distribution to \textbf{0.366}
held-out (Brier 0.281\,$\to$\,0.876); autonomous coverage at 5\% risk collapses
from 63\% to 0.6\%. Temperature scaling fit in-distribution transfers only
partially: on the shifted bench ECE falls to 0.220, far from the 0.104 oracle
refit (Table~\ref{tab:ts}; Figure~\ref{fig:rel}).
Yet error-detection AUROC falls only from 0.87 to 0.73: confidence still
separates right from wrong answers, confirming that accuracy, calibration, and
sensitivity are distinct quantities~\cite{systemswitch2026}. Normalized for
accuracy, selective quality degrades too: AURC rises from 0.053
in-distribution to 0.389 under shift on BANKING77 (CLINC150: 0.106 vs 0.144).
Within a single model (LOTO-banking), ECE rises from 0.176 on in-distribution
CLINC150 to 0.366 on the held-out taxonomy---the apples-to-apples shift pair.
With a 1\,000-item calibration slice (500 BANKING77 plus 500 CLINC150 train
rows, dump \texttt{docs/eval/kaggle/E-full/cal1k.json}), temperature
$T{=}1.72$ cuts ECE 0.119\,$\to$\,0.039 on BANKING77 and transfers to
CLINC150 (0.091\,$\to$\,0.048, oracle 0.049): at cal-1k scale the scaler
transfers where the lean-split scaler only partially did.

\subsection{Full Tests, OOS, and Latency}
On the official full tests, recipe E gives typed 58.8\% [53.9--63.5] (400),
BANKING77 \textbf{75.6\%} [74.1--77.0] (3\,080), and CLINC150 \textbf{67.6\%}
[66.3--68.9] (5\,500), with in-scope CLINC150 at 75.9\% [74.7--77.2]
(Table~\ref{tab:full}; bootstrap 95\% intervals, dump
\texttt{docs/eval/kaggle/E-full/full13.json}). The full-test numbers sit
$\sim$5\,pt below the 500-subset means (81.3/72.9): the subsets flattered the
model, and the full tests are the honest numbers. On the 1\,000-utterance OOS
slice (dump \texttt{docs/eval/kaggle/E-oos/oos13.json}), abstention at
$\tau{=}0.5$ is only \textbf{33.5\%} [30.6--36.5] with mean max-probability
0.637---the model is overconfident out of scope, so in-scope accuracy
overstates deployment autonomy where OOS must escalate.

The semantic test is decisive: permuting label names while keeping
descriptions collapses BANKING77 79.6\,$\to$\,5.6\% and CLINC150

in label-name semantics, not phrasing, which is the correct reading of the
wording-robustness grid above.

A fine-tuned encoder baseline (ModernBERT-base, 149M, same official tests,
run by us) splits the comparison (Table~\ref{tab:encoder}): the encoder wins

\subsection{Generative Baseline: Same Backbone}
Prompting the identical LFM2.5-350M weights for JSON (zero- and one-shot,
500 banking intents) yields \textbf{0.0\% accuracy at $\sim$100\% schema
violation} (183\,ms/row of unparseable output): the base backbone cannot
follow a JSON schema at all, so no same-backbone generative
accuracy/latency comparison exists to report. The decision head is what makes
this backbone usable---and the latency-vs-generative claim rests on decoding
complexity ($O(1)$ vs $O(T)$ steps), not a measurement. No 350M instruct
variant exists from the vendor to serve as the ``dominant practice''
reference, which we disclose rather than substitute across families.

\subsection{Capacity Frontier}
Typed-decisions plateaus at 54--59\% across all runs while Qwen3.5-0.8B
reports 73\%: unlike single wide taxonomies (handled well even at 150
classes), its records demand up to five interdependent judgments per state
(risk, review need, routing) that stretch 16 layers at width 1\,024. The same
recipe on LFM2-700M gives typed-ext 0.583 vs 0.549$\pm$0.011 at 350M
($+$3.4\,pt from 2$\times$ parameters), yet the gap to the vendor-reported
Qwen 73\% stays $\sim$15\,pt: scale helps a little but recipe plus scale does
not close the gap, so ``architectural capacity, not recipe'' does not hold as
stated. The honest bound is narrower: at fixed recipe the 350M hybrid tops
out on joint multi-field reasoning, bounding deployment scope to
single-question routing rather than joint multi-field reasoning.

\subsection{Discussion}
Fixed 350M capacity forces mixture trade-offs, and the precursor runs that
first surfaced them are logged with kernel IDs in Table~\ref{tab:precursors}:
v5$\to$v6 shows the 36\%\,$\to$\,83\% augmentation jump was a
context-truncation confound (v5 ran 2\,048-token context cutting wide schemas;
v6's augmentation subsampling made them fit), while v2$\to$v3 shows doubling
the typed-decisions weight relocates capacity (BANKING77 +2, CLINC150 $-$7)
rather than creating it.

For practitioners: fit schemas
in context first (truncation dwarfs recipe effects), buy augmentation for
per-bench transfer and memory rather than in-domain
points, and never trust in-distribution temperatures under shift. Deploy with
an escalation policy
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}(x) = \arg\max_k p_k(x) \text{ if } \max_k p_k(x)\ge\tau
\text{ else ESCALATE},
\end{equation}
with $\tau$ set from AUROC-validated coverage analysis (our models sustain 63\%
autonomy in-distribution at 5\% risk but near zero under shift, so $\tau$ must
be re-fit per deployment distribution). Limits: A/E are single-seed
with a batch-size confound (4$\times$8 vs 8$\times$4 at equal effective size 32,
forced by full-schema OOM); rename/paraphrase/derived types are not yet
separated, so the transfer credit cannot be attributed to one mechanism; no
same-protocol Qwen rerun exists (vendor numbers are labeled context only);
OOS abstention is measured (33.5\% at $\tau{=}0.5$, overconfident at mean
max-probability 0.637) but no abstain policy is tuned,
macro-F1 leaderboard slices are future work, and 5 of 18 mixture subsets
remain license-unaudited (Section~3); and the base model carries the
\texttt{lfm1.0} license (LiquidAI model card), so our release covers only our
own artifacts (adapters, heads, dumps, scripts) at
\url{https://huggingface.co/itsdevice/}, not redistributed base weights. How these bound the headline findings: the transfer finding now rests
on matched A/E LOTO cells per bench (augmentation buys BANKING77, nothing on
CLINC)---and it
sits at or below a zero-shot embedding baseline on both taxonomies, so
``substantial'' overstates it; the
in-distribution comparison is a null result (1.4\,pt inside 7.6\,pt seed
spread with a batch-size confound), so it
is consistent with seed noise; the calibration finding holds within matched
models, with scaler transfer now measured as partial on lean splits
(0.366\,$\to$\,0.220 vs
0.104 oracle) but transferring at cal-1k scale (0.119\,$\to$\,0.039 bank,
0.091\,$\to$\,0.048 clinc). Each rests
on fixed test sets with disclosed residuals.
