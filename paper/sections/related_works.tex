\section{Related Works}
\subsection{System-One Decision Models}
TypeSafe's Jev frames automation as decision routing with honest
probabilities~\cite{typesafe-jev}. Cloudflare's Clef (27B) and Clef-flash (9B)
pair frozen Qwen backbones with a joint schema head trained with label-smoothed
cross-entropy, Brier penalty, and an RLCD secondary objective; weights are open
under Apache-2.0~\cite{cloudflare-clef}. Convai's Laya shows sub-500M
bidirectional encoders reaching single-digit millisecond latency~\cite{convai-laya}.
LiquidAI's open d1 family (d1-omni-600M, same LFM lineage as our backbone)
provides the closest size-class reference~\cite{liquid-d1}. We evaluate
against vendor-reported numbers only, clearly labeled, since rerunning 9--27B
models is outside a T4 protocol.

\subsection{Which RLCD?}
 We use RLCD as \textit{Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions}
 (TypeSafe/Cloudflare: RL with a proper-scoring-rule reward), unrelated to
 Yang et~al.'s contrastive-distillation variant~\cite{yang2024rlcd}; the
 reference-KL term defaults to zero and is unused here, so RLCD is a
 hypothesis for future work, not a method used in this paper.

\subsection{Calibration and Selective Classification}
A scoring rule is strictly proper if truth-telling uniquely maximizes the
expected score~\cite{gneiting2007strictly}. The Brier score~\cite{brier1950verification} and its
reliability--resolution--uncertainty decomposition~\cite{murphy1973new} are our main
tools. Guo et~al. showed modern networks are overconfident and temperature
scaling helps~\cite{guo2017calibration}. Selective classification formalizes
abstention via coverage and selective risk~\cite{geifman2017selective}; our
coverage-at-risk metric follows that line.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Decision Benchmarks}
Our intent benches are BANKING77~\cite{casanueva2020banking77} (77 intents,
banking) and CLINC150~\cite{larson2019clinc150} (150 intents plus
out-of-scope). TypedBench proposes accuracy, paraphrase robustness, calibration against a
finite-sample noise floor, and selective prediction for decision
models~\cite{typedbench2026}; we borrow its paraphrase-grid and selective
protocol. System Switch shows accuracy, ECE, and error-detection AUROC are
distinct quantities, with deferral gain tracking AUROC
($\rho=0.87$)~\cite{systemswitch2026}---exactly the dissociation we observe
under shift. Calibration degradation under dataset shift is documented
since~\cite{ovadia2019trust}; our shift slice adds the matched-recipe
dissociation (AUROC intact while ECE collapses) plus a calibrated
temperature-transfer measurement. Small-model precedents include distillation from System-One
labels~\cite{distilljev2026}. Head-to-head benchmarking finds small decision
models beating small generative ones on knowledge tasks while losing on
math~\cite{jevbench2026}, which bounds where our 350M claims apply: intent
and routing taxonomies, not reasoning benchmarks. We disclose that classic
few-shot intent baselines---ConvFiT, SetFit, and zero-shot NLI/embedding
classifiers---were not run: our only trained-vs-untrained comparisons are
against our own fine-tuned ModernBERT encoder and a zero-shot embedding
baseline, so no claim is made against the few-shot literature. Companion efforts cover
moderation confidence benchmarking~\cite{modbench2026}, meta-encoding and
knowledge-tracing variants~\cite{metaenc2026,jevkt2026}. Option-order sensitivity
motivates schema augmentation as a treatment~\cite{pezeshkpour2024option}.
